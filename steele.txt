Here’s the story behind the subway map:

When I was working on the Scheme-78 chip (part of the first
multiproject chip for the Fall 1978 Mead-Conway VLSI course at MIT),
for the last few weeks I was working _very_ long hours and not getting
much sleep. Late one evening, as I was riding home on the MBTA, I was
blearily staring at the subway map on the wall of the subway car, this
thought crossed my mind: “How about that? Park Street [station] is a
transistor!” (Because that is where the MBTA Red Line crosses the
Green Line, and in the Mead-Conway CMOS design formalism lines in the
diffusion layer are coded green and lines of polysilicon are coded
red, and transistors are formed where a run of polysilicon crosses a
run of diffusion.)

When we did the Scheme-79 chip, the other three designers did all the
essential layout work (I was too busy working on my PhD dissertation),
but they did kindly offer me the opportunity to contribute the
identifying logo artwork. I wanted of course to identify What, Who,
When, and Where:

What: Scheme chip

Who: the four designers and their affiliations at MIT and Xerox

When: July 1979

Where: well, I couldn’t resist the opportunity to actually render Park
Street as a transistor, so I drew the entire Boston subway map as it
was in 1979 (I had to use red for both Red Line and Orange Line). If
you look carefully, you will see one extra dot of blue on the left,
between Central Square and Kendall Square, indicating the location of
545 Technology Square, where Gerry and Jack and I had offices.

I believe I briefly considered also trying to render the California
BART map, but it didn't really extend anywhere close to the Xerox PARC
building, so I gave up on that idea.

I also have a vague memory that Jack and Alan had to temporarily
delete the logo from the layout because the fact that Park Street was
a lone transistor that didn’t connect sensibly to anything else upset
the design-rule-checking software. :-)
